Outsourcing structural steel detailing works best when you hand over a defined package: agreed inputs, a deliverables list, a stated responsibility split, a review cycle and a handover format. Your team keeps design authority and approval, and the subcontractor supplies production capacity. Most outsourcing problems trace back to undefined scope, not to distance or time zones.

## When outsourcing detailing makes sense

Outsourcing tends to work well when:

- the in-house team is at peak load and the backlog is delaying fabrication;
- packages are well bounded, such as a pipe rack, a set of equipment platforms, or a skid-steel package;
- the design is stable enough to detail, or can be split into frozen areas;
- you can name a single technical point of contact on your side.

It is harder when:

- the design is still moving daily and decisions are made informally on site;
- the detailer is expected to resolve design questions without access to the engineer;
- nobody owns the review of returned drawings.

## Define the package before you approach suppliers

Before requesting quotations, write a one-page package definition:

| Item | Example entry |
|---|---|
| Scope boundary | Pipe rack PR-01 to PR-04, including secondary steel for pipe supports; excludes stairs |
| Inputs available | IFC structural drawings rev C, piping support loads rev B, vendor data pending for two items |
| Deliverables | Tekla model, assembly and single-part drawings, erection GA, bolt list, MTO |
| Responsibility | Connections per project standard details; non-standard connections designed by EPC structural team |
| Standards | Project specification; drawing standard per client procedure |
| Software and formats | Tekla Structures (version to be agreed); deliver native, IFC, PDF, DWG, NC/DSTV |
| Review | Client comments within an agreed number of working days, one consolidated comment set per issue |
| Milestones | Anchor plans first, then rack by rack |

This definition becomes the basis of your RFQ (see our guide on [writing an RFQ for a detailing package](/resources/how-to-write-an-rfq-for-structural-detailing)) and later the statement of work.

## How to evaluate a detailing subcontractor

Price matters, but it is a poor predictor of fabrication outcomes on its own. A simple weighted scorecard helps:

| Criterion | What to ask for | Evidence to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant experience | Examples of similar structure types (pipe racks, platforms, modules) | Sanitised sample drawings; clear distinction between company work and individuals' prior employment |
| Scope understanding | Their questions after reading your package | Specific RFIs and assumptions, not just a price |
| Responsibility clarity | How they treat connection design and approvals | Written boundaries, no blanket "we design everything" |
| Checking process | How drawings are checked before issue | Named checking step, checklists, separation of modeller and checker |
| Revision control | How revisions and superseded sheets are handled | Drawing register, revision clouds, transmittals |
| Software and formats | Version compatibility and exchange formats | Confirmation of the version and a test exchange |
| Communication | Point of contact, working hours overlap, query turnaround | Named lead; agreed query log |
| Data security | How your files are stored and shared | NDA willingness; controlled file transfer |

Be cautious about claims that cannot be checked: "error-free", "100% accuracy", or savings percentages given without a stated basis. For a sense of how we handle checking, see our [quality and delivery approach](/quality-delivery).

## Managing the work

1. **Kickoff.** Walk through the inputs, the responsibility split, priorities and the drawing standard. Agree the query log format.
2. **Queries.** Batch RFIs daily or weekly. Answer in writing, and record answers in the log so they are not lost in email or chat.
3. **Staged issues.** Ask for a sample assembly and erection drawing early, so you can correct conventions before the whole package is dressed.
4. **Reviews.** Consolidate comments from engineering, fabrication and QA into a single return per issue. Conflicting comments from different reviewers are a common cause of churn.
5. **Revisions.** Require revision letters, clouds and an updated register with every re-issue.

## Handover and close-out

At completion, expect native model files (if contracted), PDFs, DWGs, reports, NC data, a final drawing register and a list of open items or assumptions. Confirm model ownership and licence compatibility in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does outsourcing transfer design responsibility?**
No, unless explicitly contracted to an appropriately qualified party. The usual arrangement is that the client's engineering team retains design and approval authority.

**How do we handle time-zone differences?**
Agree a daily overlap window and a written query log. A clear package definition matters more than real-time availability.

**Can we start with a small trial package?**
Yes. A single rack bay or platform is a practical way to test conventions, communication and quality before awarding more.
